How to correctly report a peony

This guide helps surveyors submit useful and verifiable reports. In this text, the terms “survey form”, “app” and “survey” refer to the same tool used to collect reports.

Open the survey form Photo guide

🌸 What to look for

Report only plants that you think are compatible with a wild peony. The report will then be checked through the photos.

✓
Large, showy flower, generally pink, red, purple or whitish, not small like a common poppy.
✓
Leaves divided into broad lobes, with a different appearance from the thin or very jagged leaves of many other herbaceous species.
✓
Robust herbaceous plant, often isolated or in small groups.

🚫 What to avoid

Do not submit random reports of red or pink flowers if the plant is not clearly visible.

×
Do not confuse with poppies: poppies have thin petals, a more slender stem and different leaves.
×
Do not submit photos taken from too far away where the plant cannot be identified.
×
Do not photograph people, license plates or private homes, unless unavoidable.

📷 The 4 required photographs

Photos are essential to confirm that the report really refers to a peony. Each report requires four different images.

🏞️

1. General photo of the site

Frame the environment around the plant: meadow, woodland, clearing, slope, path or general context.

🌿

2. Group of plants

Show the group or area where several individuals are present, if there are multiple plants nearby.

🌸

3. Largest plant

Take a clear photo of a single plant, preferably the whole plant, including stem, leaves and flower if present.

🍃

4. Underside of the leaf

Move close to a leaf and photograph its underside. This helps botanical verification.

🔎 Photo examples to identify the plant

The following images are useful examples for understanding which plant to photograph. Some examples correspond directly to the photos required by the survey, while others are only references to help recognise the peony in its different stages and plant parts.

Site context

Shows the general environment: woodland, clearing, meadow, slope or surrounding area.

Group of plants

Useful for estimating the presence of several individuals and seeing their distribution at the site.

Flower

Useful for distinguishing peonies from poppies or other pink/red flowers.

Leaf, upper side

Shows the shape, veins and general appearance of the leaf.

Leaf, underside

This is one of the photos required in the survey to help botanical verification.

Fruit / post-flowering

Can be useful when the plant is no longer in full bloom.

📝 How the survey works

Enter the surveyor’s name

You can enter the first name and surname you provided during registration.

Tap “Acquire position”

The phone automatically records latitude and longitude, but remember to enable location services. A copy of the coordinates will appear.

Indicate the estimated number of visible plants

Choose one of the available classes: 1-9, 10-19, 20-29 or more than 30.

Take the four photographs

Tap each photo box and take the requested image.

Submit the report

If there is no network connection, the data are saved locally and you can synchronise them when you are back online, simply by accessing the survey form.

📡 If there is no network connection

The survey can save reports locally. A message will appear at the bottom of the app showing the number of pending reports.

1
Fill in the report normally.
2
If a network error appears, the report is saved locally.
3
When the internet connection is restored, the reports are sent automatically. Otherwise, tap Sync local cache.

🔒 Data confidentiality

The precise location of the reports is not shown on a public map. Coordinates are collected for scientific verification, monitoring and site management, but are not freely disclosed.

For any information requests or reports, please contact the project representative.
